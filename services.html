Consulting

We help animal care organizations address the emotional realities of caring for animals. Working with leaders and staff, we identify sources of moral stress, grief, and workplace strain and develop practical supports that strengthen teams and help people remain connected to the work that matters to them.

Every consultation is tailored to the unique needs, culture, and goals of your organization. Projects begin by understanding your specific environment, staff composition, and the types of animal death and euthanasia your team encounters regularly.

Consulting areas include:

  • Staff wellness assessment and gap analysis
  • Grief-informed policy development
  • Euthanasia protocol review and team support structures
  • Secondary trauma identification and building support structures
  • Leadership training on managing moral stress in direct reports
  • Retention strategy development tied to staff sustainability

Individual support services

Support for people navigating pet loss, anticipatory grief, difficult medical or end-of-life decisions, and caregiving stress. BLUE Dog offers a free 15-minute consultation to explore whether services are a good fit.

Consultation sessions are available in 30-, 45-, and 60-minute formats to individuals nationwide. Sessions provide a structured opportunity to ask clinical questions, review the situation, and identify specific next steps.

The grief and stress associated with pet loss and end-of-life decisions are frequently minimized in standard clinical settings. These sessions provide a structured, clinically informed approach to navigating that process.

Case consultation

When clients bring animal loss, euthanasia trauma, or veterinary and shelter work experiences into session, specialized clinical knowledge is required to respond effectively.

BLUE dog provides case consultation for therapists, counselors, and social workers encountering animal loss, euthanasia trauma, and animal welfare grief in their practice.

Case consultation covers:

  • Clinical approaches to animal loss and human-animal bond grief
  • Euthanasia trauma in clients who are animal care providers
  • Secondary trauma in veterinary and shelter worker clients
  • Specialized grief models applicable to animal loss
